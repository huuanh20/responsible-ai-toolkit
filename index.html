What Is AI & What Can It Do?

AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini can draft emails, summarize documents, analyze data, and answer questions — but they are assistants, not decision-makers.

Beginner
⚠️

What NOT to Share with AI

Never input customer personal data (PII), confidential contracts, internal financial reports, or any data marked "Confidential" into public AI tools.

Critical
✅

Approved Use Cases at Work

Drafting internal communications, brainstorming ideas, summarizing public articles, creating presentation outlines, and translating documents.

Approved
🔍

Always Verify AI Output

AI can "hallucinate" — generate confident but incorrect information. Always fact-check key figures, names, dates, and legal statements before using them.

Important
⚖️

AI Bias & Fairness

AI systems may reflect biases from their training data. Be especially careful with AI-generated content involving people, demographics, or sensitive decisions.

Ethics
📋

Your Accountability

You are responsible for any AI-generated content you submit or publish. Review all outputs before sharing. AI is a tool — you are the professional.

📄 Reports

Executive Summary

"Summarize the following content into a 3-paragraph executive summary for senior management. Focus on: key findings, business impact, and recommended next steps. Content: [paste content]"
📄 Reports

Weekly Status Report

"Write a professional weekly status report based on these bullet points: [list tasks]. Format it with sections: Completed This Week, In Progress, Blockers, Next Week Plan."
📊 Analysis

SWOT Analysis

"Conduct a SWOT analysis for [topic/project/idea]. Present it in a clear table with Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. Context: [brief description]"
📊 Analysis

Compare Options

"Compare [Option A] and [Option B] across these criteria: [cost, time, risk, etc.]. Present as a comparison table. Conclude with a recommendation and brief rationale."
🎯 Meetings

Meeting Agenda

"Create a structured meeting agenda for a [duration]-minute meeting on [topic]. Include: objective, attendees, agenda items with time slots, and expected outcomes."
🎯 Meetings

Meeting Minutes

"Convert these meeting notes into formal minutes: [paste raw notes]. Format with: Date, Attendees, Key Decisions, Action Items (Owner + Deadline), Next Meeting."
✍️ Content

Simplify Jargon

"Rewrite the following technical/legal text so a non-specialist with no background in [field] can understand it easily. Use simple language, short sentences, and analogies where helpful: [text]"

Presentation Outline

FAQ
Common Questions About AI at Work
Answers to the most frequently asked questions from employees new to AI tools.
Can I use ChatGPT / Gemini for work tasks? +
Yes, for many tasks — but with important boundaries. You may use public AI tools for drafting, brainstorming, summarizing public information, and creating templates. You must NOT input any confidential company data, customer PII, or sensitive documents into these tools, as their data may be used to train future models.
Is AI output always accurate? +
No. AI tools can "hallucinate" — meaning they can generate confident-sounding but completely incorrect information. Always verify facts, figures, names, dates, and any technical or legal content before using it in professional contexts. Think of AI as a fast first draft, not a final source of truth.
What data am I not allowed to put into AI tools? +
Never input: (1) Customer personal information (name, ID, address, phone), (2) Internal financial data, (3) Contracts or legal documents marked confidential, (4) Employee personal records, (5) Proprietary business strategies or unreleased product information. When in doubt, ask your IT or compliance team.
Who is responsible if AI-generated content is wrong? +
You are. As the employee who uses and submits the content, you bear professional responsibility for its accuracy and appropriateness. AI is a tool, not an independent agent. Always review, verify, and approve all AI output before sending or publishing it.
Can AI make biased decisions? +
Yes. AI systems are trained on large datasets that may contain historical biases. This can lead to biased outputs, especially in tasks involving people evaluation, hiring suggestions, or demographic descriptions. Always apply human judgment to any AI output involving decisions about people.
What is "Responsible AI"? +
Responsible AI is a framework for developing and using AI in a way that is fair, transparent, accountable, and safe. It means ensuring AI tools don't discriminate, that humans remain in control of important decisions, and that privacy and security are protected. Companies like FWD adopt Responsible AI principles to build trust with customers and regulators.
Does using AI mean my job is at risk? +
AI is designed to augment human work, not replace skilled professionals. Employees who learn to use AI effectively become more productive and more valuable. The risk is greater for those who resist learning these tools. Think of AI like email or Excel — a productivity tool that became standard in the workplace.
Can AI-generated content have copyright issues? +
Potentially, yes. AI models are trained on internet content and may reproduce patterns from copyrighted works. For creative or published content (marketing copy, articles, etc.), always review outputs carefully and add significant human editing. Consult Legal if publishing externally.
What should I do if I'm unsure about an AI use case? +
Ask before you act. Contact your IT Governance team or line manager to verify whether a specific use case is approved. It's always better to check than to risk a data or compliance breach.
How do I write a better prompt to get better AI output? +
Follow this simple formula: Role + Task + Context + Format. Example: "You are a professional business writer [Role]. Draft a follow-up email [Task] for a client meeting about insurance renewal held on May 5th [Context]. Keep it under 100 words and professional in tone [Format]." More specific prompts = much better results.
Safety Framework
5 Steps to Use AI Safely at Work
A simple checklist every employee should follow before using AI for any work task.
1

🔍 Identify the task

Is this task suitable for AI? Good fits: drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, translating. Poor fits: making financial decisions, accessing customer records, legal judgments.

2

🔒 Check your data

Before typing anything into an AI tool, ask: "Does this contain confidential, personal, or sensitive information?" If yes — stop. Use only anonymized or public information.

3

✍️ Write a clear prompt

Use the Role + Task + Context + Format framework. Specific prompts get better results. Vague prompts get vague, potentially unusable outputs.

4

✅ Review and verify all output

Read the AI's response critically. Check facts, figures, and logic. Edit to match your voice and ensure accuracy. Never copy-paste AI output without reviewing it first.

5

📝 Document your AI use if required

Some teams or compliance frameworks require logging when AI was used to produce content. Check with your manager or IT governance team on your department's policy.
